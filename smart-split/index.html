Canvas A · Start with the market

Section 1 / 2 min

Category

Group payments & shared-money infrastructure.

Adjacent — but not — Splitwise (tracking), Venmo (P2P), QuickBooks (accounting). Smart Split is "togetherness infrastructure for shared money."

Who

Urban Gen-Z & younger millennials, 22–32, who eat out, travel, or share housing in groups of 3–8.

Sub-segment first: trip groups of 4–8 — peak pain density, peak repeat use.

Urgent problem

Shared spend creates social friction. Existing tools either track (Splitwise) or pay (Venmo) — never both with fairness intelligence.

What makes it painful now: post-pandemic social re-emergence + inflation = higher shared spend, lower tolerance for friction.

Stay above feature talk. The category sentence is the most important sentence in the whole deck — it tells listeners what we are NOT.

Canvas A · Why it matters & what they do today

Section 1 / cont.

Motivations (Jobs-to-be-Done)

  • Emotional — end the night without owing anyone.
  • Social — protect group harmony.
  • Operational — drop the mental ledger.
  • Economic — stop quietly losing money in forgotten reimbursements.

Job statement: "Help me close the tab on this group, so we can keep being friends."

Current alternatives

  • Splitwise — tracks; never settles.
  • Venmo / WeChat Pay — settles; never computes fairly.
  • Spreadsheets & mental math — most common, lowest fidelity.
  • "One person eats it" — silent equity tax on the most generous friend.

Workaround proves problem: 71% of our 28 interviewees use ≥2 tools per trip.

Switching forces

Pushing them away from the old way: cognitive load, embarrassment, lost money, broken evenings.

Anxiety / friction that could stop adoption:

  • Trust to connect a wallet.
  • "Is the split actually fair?"
  • Group adoption — no value if only one person uses it.
Frame motivations as jobs, not features. The switching-forces column is where you signal you understand the adoption math, not just the problem.

Canvas A · Success, meaning, position

Section 1 / land it

Success trigger

What we will see when value lands:

  • "I forgot we even split it." (qualitative)
  • ≥2 repeats within 7 days (behavioral).
  • ≥2 group members invited per active user.

New meaning

Splitting is no longer accounting between friends.

It is a quiet act of care that makes the money disappear so the relationship can stay.

Not just "AI for receipts" — the feature is the enabler of a meaning shift.

Innovation position

Meaning-driven (Verganti) with AI as enabler.

We are not racing Splitwise on features. We are reframing what splitting means — from ledger to togetherness — and using AI to make the new meaning frictionless.

Why this quadrant

The math of splitting has been solved for a decade — Splitwise nailed it. What has not been solved is the social meaning around the math: the awkwardness, the unspoken debts, the friendship tax.

Smart Split changes meaning first, then uses AI (OCR + group context) only to make the new meaning frictionless.

Risk this frame surfaces

If users only hear "AI receipt scanner," we have collapsed into the tech-push quadrant. Our pitch, onboarding, and metrics must protect the meaning frame.

Use Verganti to give peers a vocabulary for critique: are we really meaning-driven, or are we wearing meaning as a coat over a tech-push product? Invite that question.

Balfour · The Four Fits, applied

Theory frame 2
1

Market ↔ Product

Trip groups, 22–32. High frequency, high social stakes. Product solves emotional + operational problem — not just the math.

2

Product ↔ Channel

Product is the channel. Every split is a group invite. Viral coefficient = built into the JTBD; zero paid acquisition at seed.

3

Channel ↔ Model

Viral channels demand zero-friction monetisation → freemium. Free for casual groups; premium for multi-currency, AI auto-categorise, trip mode.

4

Model ↔ Market

$4–6/mo per power user matches young-pro willingness to pay for social peace. Transaction-fee tier for high-volume travel groups.

→   if any one fit is loose, growth leaks out of it. Balfour's argument: it's a system, not a checklist.   ←

Tightest fit (today)

Product ↔ Channel. Every successful split creates an invite event. We have early evidence this works (see customer evidence).

Loosest fit (today)

Channel ↔ Model. We don't yet know if freemium converts at the rate viral channels demand. This is our biggest open question past prototype.

Balfour is the discipline check: meaning is romance, fits are math. Name the loosest fit honestly — that's where peer feedback is most useful.

Customer evidence checkpoint

Section 3 / cont.

Strongest validation signal

8 / 10 concierge testers invited a friend during the test session — unprompted.

6 / 10 settled with real money in v0.3, not just simulated.

Strongest contradiction

4 / 10 first-time testers paused at the payment-connect step. Trust gap is real, not theoretical.

Older sub-segment hard-stopped at the same step — confirms segment focus, but also that the trust UX matters for everyone.

Customer behaviour to watch next

  • Day-7 repeat use rate.
  • Invites sent per active user (viral coefficient).
  • Settle-rate — % of splits that complete payment, not just compute.
"Settle rate" is the metric we want peers to remember. Compute-only is the old meaning; settle is the new meaning expressed as a number.

Feedback ask · the riskiest assumption

Section 4 / 2 min

Riskiest assumption

Users will connect a payment method on first use (trust), and the meaning shift — care, not accounting — drives adoption over feature comparison.

If either half is wrong, the whole "settle in one tap" promise collapses into a better Splitwise.

Where the prototype logic is weak

  • Trust-on-day-one. Should v1 ship without payment connect?
  • Whether "togetherness" survives translation into a 60-character app-store subtitle.
  • Whether the freemium price point is below the threshold of "worth the friction."

Specifically, we are asking peers for:

  • What evidence in our deck challenged the "togetherness > accounting" framing?
  • In the trip-group test — is the buyer (trip organiser) the same person as the user (group)? If not, what does that change?
  • Why aren't we charging at the receipt step? (Stress-test our pricing intuition.)
Be specific about what you want peers to NOT do: don't suggest features. Do challenge the meaning frame and the fits. Quote the listener principle if needed.

Decision after feedback & one-week commitment

Section 4 / close

Decision rules after feedback

  • Persevere if ≥40% of new trip-segment users connect a wallet in session 1.
  • Refine if 20–40% connect and invite-rate ≥1.5 — decouple trust from value (delay payment connect).
  • Pivot user if <20% connect — move from "settlement" to "split-intelligence-only" for a more trusting segment.
  • Pivot meaning if invites <1 per user — the togetherness frame isn't landing; rebuild it.

One-week iteration commitment

By next class we will test: payment-connect rate with 10 new trip-segment users on v0.4.

Minimum evidence we will bring back:

  • Connect rate (target ≥40%).
  • Settle rate (target ≥60% of connecters).
  • 3+ verbatim quotes on trust hesitation.
  • One short clip of the most confusing moment.
Reading the persevere/refine/pivot rules aloud is the single highest-credibility move in the whole deck. It signals you will actually act on evidence.
